# English AI writing patterns and alternatives

This reference catalog maps common AI-generated English patterns to clean, human-written alternatives. Use it only when the rewrite task needs a concrete vocabulary or style check.

## 1. Significance and trend inflation

- **Keywords**: `serves as`, `stands as a testament`, `vital`, `significant`, `crucial`, `pivotal`, `key role`, `represents a shift`, `evolving landscape`, `focal point`, `indelible mark`, `deeply rooted`, `setting the stage for`
- **Reason**: AI text often inflates ordinary facts with grand contextual statements.
- **Rewrite direction**: Replace the inflated framing with a concrete fact or direct purpose.

## 2. Present participle tack-ons

- **Keywords**: `highlighting`, `underscoring`, `emphasizing`, `ensuring`, `reflecting`, `symbolizing`, `contributing to`, `cultivating`, `fostering`, `encompassing`, `showcasing`
- **Reason**: AI text often appends participle phrases to create a false sense of depth.
- **Rewrite direction**: Split the sentence, make the relationship explicit, or remove the tack-on.

## 3. High-frequency AI vocabulary

- **Keywords**: `Additionally`, `align with`, `crucial`, `delve`, `emphasizing`, `enduring`, `enhance`, `fostering`, `garner`, `interplay`, `intricate`, `intricacies`, `tapestry`, `testament`, `underscore`, `valuable`, `vibrant`
- **Reason**: These words cluster in generated text and make prose feel generic.
- **Rewrite direction**: Use the specific noun, verb, or fact the sentence actually needs.

## 4. Copula avoidance

- **Keywords**: `serves as`, `stands as`, `marks`, `represents`, `boasts`, `features`, `offers`
- **Reason**: AI text often avoids direct `is`, `are`, or `has` constructions.
- **Rewrite direction**: Use plain verbs when they are clearer.

## 5. Overused rhetorical structures

- **Rule of three**: Avoid forcing every claim into three items.
- **Negative parallelism**: Reduce `not only... but also...` and `it is not just... it is...` unless the contrast is genuinely useful.

## 6. Layout and punctuation artifacts

- Reduce em dash overuse in formal text.
- Remove decorative emojis unless the genre calls for them.
- Prefer straight quotes in technical/plain text contexts.
- Use sentence case unless the target style guide requires title case.
- Avoid lists where every item starts with a bold label and a colon.

## 7. Document-shape artifacts

- Check for paragraphs with nearly identical length and sentence count.
- Break repeated claim-evidence-lesson endings when the source material does not naturally follow that arc.
- Remove exhaustive, checklist-like coverage that gives minor points the same weight as the main argument.
- Vary structure only when it reflects the writer's real emphasis; random variation is not a goal.

## 8. Meaning preservation and over-editing guard

- Lock claims, names, numbers, dates, negation, conditions, agency, and causal direction before rewriting.
- Do not strengthen `may help` into `solves`, weaken `must` into `should`, or move responsibility to a different actor.
- Leave already-natural sentences alone. Preserve voice and genre over lexical novelty.
